CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION & PROBLEM STATEMENT
1.1 BACKGROUND & MOTIVATION
Traditional retail marketing relies on generic broadcast channels (email and SMS).
Email marketing suffers from sub-3% click-through rates and high spam filtering.
Conversely, conversational messaging platforms like WhatsApp achieve open rates exceeding
90% and engagement within 15 minutes of receipt.

1.2 THE RESEARCH PROBLEM & CORE CHALLENGE
Despite WhatsApp's high engagement, brands face severe challenges:
1. Lack of Automated Behavioral Segmentation: Marketers use static lists or crude demographics
   rather than real purchasing behavior.
2. The "Marketing Fatigue" Paradox: Because WhatsApp is an intimate personal channel,
   over-messaging rapidly leads to user annoyance, high opt-outs (>15%), and brand damage.
3. Privacy & Compliance Non-Adherence: Existing tools fail to enforce rolling frequency caps,
   explicit consent verification, and suppression list matching prior to dispatch.
4. "Black-Box" ML: Enterprise recommendation systems rarely provide explainable rationales
   for why a customer received a specific promotional offer.

1.3 PROJECT OBJECTIVES
- Objective 1: Build a resilient ingestion engine that cleans raw invoices, isolating returns
  and removing corrupted rows.
- Objective 2: Engineer Recency, Frequency, and Monetary (RFM) behavioral vectors per customer.
- Objective 3: Implement unsupervised K-Means clustering with automated hyperparameter sweeps
  (Silhouette Score, Davies-Bouldin Index, Calinski-Harabasz Index).
- Objective 4: Dynamically assign descriptive behavioral labels (e.g., "Loyal High-Value",
  "Recent Frequent", "Inactive Low-Value") based on centroid percentiles.
- Objective 5: Enforce a strict 3-tier Marketing Fatigue Engine (Consent check, Suppression check,
  Rolling 30-day Frequency Capping) before message queuing.
- Objective 6: Generate explainable campaign recommendations paired with plain-English rationale.
- Objective 7: Provide an interactive React/FastAPI dashboard with a safe simulated WhatsApp preview.

CHAPTER 2: MATHEMATICAL FORMULATIONS & METHODOLOGY
2.1 RFM FEATURE EXTRACTION
For a dataset of invoices over period T with reference anchor date t_ref:
- Recency (R_i): Days elapsed since customer i's most recent transaction:
    R_i = (t_ref - max(t_ij)) where t_ij is the date of invoice j for customer i.
- Frequency (F_i): Count of distinct, valid purchasing visits:
    F_i = count(distinct invoice_id_ij)
- Monetary (M_i): Total net monetary value of customer i's transactions:
    M_i = sum(quantity_ijk * unit_price_ijk) for non-cancellation lines.

2.2 SKEWNESS REDUCTION & SCALING
Retail transactional spend follows a heavy right-tailed Pareto distribution. To prevent
monetary values from dominating Euclidean distance in K-Means:
1. Logarithmic Transformation:
    M'_i = ln(M_i + 1)
2. Robust / Z-Score Standardization:
    z = (x - mu) / sigma
    Resulting in scaled 3D vectors X_i = [R_scaled, F_scaled, M_log_scaled].

2.3 K-MEANS OPTIMIZATION & METRIC SWEEPS
K-Means minimizes the within-cluster sum of squares (inertia):
    J = sum_{k=1}^K sum_{x in C_k} || x - mu_k ||^2

Hyperparameter Selection Metrics:
- Silhouette Coefficient s(i) = (b(i) - a(i)) / max(a(i), b(i)) in [-1, +1].
- Davies-Bouldin Index DBI = (1/K) sum_{i=1}^K max_{j != i} ( (s_i + s_j) / d(mu_i, mu_j) )
- Calinski-Harabasz Index (Variance Ratio Criterion): Between-cluster dispersion over within-cluster dispersion.
